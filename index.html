How it works

  1. Fifty CVs that all sound the same.

    Everyone writes with the same tools, so every CV says the same things. Maria cannot tell who can do what.

  2. A vague ask becomes a plain card.

    Maria says she needs a cybersecurity person. A short chat turns that into three real tasks and the skills they use.

  3. Ahmed answers in his own words.

    Two practical questions about Maria’s real need. His answers become an evidence card. There is no score.

  4. Elina decides. Then they talk.

    She sees a suggestion, the reason and a question to ask. A person clicks Accept or Skip. Nothing happens without that click.

Companies ask too late and too vaguely. CVs all sound alike. Hallway gets both sides to say what they mean first.

CVs now read the same. Answers do not.

Most people write their CV with the same AI tools, so the lines blur together. Hallway asks something a CV cannot: what would you do on day one of this real job?

A candidate can still use AI to write an answer, so the card starts a conversation and a person follows up face to face. These names and answers are made up.

Responsible by design

Hallway helps people talk to each other. It does not decide anything about them.

A person decides

  • Every match is accepted or skipped by a human.
  • No scores, no rankings of people.
  • No automated decisions or profiling about a person.

Little data, all made up

  • Synthetic data only. Nobody here is real.
  • Minimal personal data: a name, country, languages, education, the support someone asks for, and a few short answers.
  • A fairness panel shows who is suggested, by country, language and education, against the whole pool. Blind review hides names and pictures until a person accepts.

The EU AI Act, in plain words

The Act puts AI that helps choose people for jobs in its high-risk group. So Hallway only suggests, shows its reasons, keeps a person in charge and checks its own spread.

This is a hackathon prototype, not legal advice or a compliance claim.

Honest limits

A candidate can use AI to write an answer.

So an evidence card is a place to start talking, not proof of skill. The person running the event asks a follow-up face to face. Suggestions can also be wrong or lopsided, which is why every one can be skipped.
