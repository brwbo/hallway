Privacy and the EU AI Act

What Hallway keeps, where it lives, and how it lines up with the rules for AI that helps choose people for jobs. This is a hackathon prototype, so it is not legal advice and not a compliance claim.

What Hallway keeps

Only what a conversation needs. Everyone in the demo is made up.

ItemWhy it is neededWhere it livesHow long

Follow-up notesSo the organiser can write to people afterwards.This browser.Until you delete it.

In a pilot these move to Espoo’s own systems, and the retention period is set with Espoo’s data protection officer.

Hallway never collects photos, voice, faces, location, scores, rankings or any guess about how someone feels.

The pages call small server functions that read text and store nothing. When an AI key is set on the server, the card or answers are sent to that AI provider to write cards, questions and suggestions. This public demo runs without one.

The EU AI Act, feature by feature

AI that helps select people for jobs is high risk under the Act. Here is what Hallway already does, and what a real pilot would still need.

RequirementWhat Hallway doesWhat a pilot still needs
Human oversightA named person accepts or skips every match. Nothing happens on its own.Training for organisers, and a way to stop the system.
TransparencyEach suggestion shows its reason. The company page says it is an AI assistant, and the site says when answers are scripted.Plain notices for candidates before they take part.
Record keepingThe decision log shows who decided what, and when.Logs kept on Espoo’s systems for a set period.
Data and biasA fairness panel compares who is suggested with the whole pool. Blind review hides names and pictures until a person accepts. Country, language and education never pick anyone.Testing on real, consented data with Espoo before use.
AccuracyCode checks that every quote on an evidence card is the candidate’s own words. A scripted fallback keeps it working if the AI fails. Automated tests cover the server.Testing at real events, and a plan for mistakes.
Banned usesNo emotion recognition, no biometric identification and no social scoring.Keeping it that way.
The rest of the high-risk rulesNot done in this prototype.Risk management, technical documentation, a conformity assessment, registration, a data protection impact assessment, and Espoo’s duties as the organisation using it.
